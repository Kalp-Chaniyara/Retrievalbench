# Company History & Sustainability

## Founding

Aurora Coffee Roasters was founded in 2014 in Portland, Oregon by Mara Velez
and Tomas Idris. The two met while working at a small third-wave coffee bar
and bonded over a shared frustration: specialty coffee bags rarely told you
where the beans actually came from. They started Aurora out of a rented
roastery space with a single 5-kilogram drum roaster and a list of eight
producer relationships built during Tomas's years buying green coffee in
Central America and East Africa.

## Early years (2014–2017)

The first three years were spent almost entirely on sourcing, not
marketing. Mara and Tomas traveled to Kenya, Ethiopia, and Colombia each
harvest season to visit the farms directly rather than buying through
brokers. By 2017, Aurora had grown from eight to nineteen direct producer
relationships and moved into a larger roastery in Portland's Central
Eastside industrial district, where the company is still headquartered
today.

## Growth and the traceability program (2018–2021)

In 2018 Aurora launched its traceability program: every bag sold since then
lists the farm of origin, the harvest year, and the name of the importer who
handled the lot. This is the practice the company's mission statement now
points to directly — making single-origin specialty coffee accessible
without sacrificing traceability. The program required Aurora to renegotiate
every producer contract on a yearly basis instead of locking into multi-year
supply agreements, which increased the company's exposure to price
volatility but let it walk away from any relationship that didn't meet its
sourcing standards.

Aurora became a certified B Corporation in 2021, formalizing practices around
producer pay, packaging, and environmental impact that had been informal
company policy since the beginning.

## Sourcing standards

Aurora pays a minimum of $3.50 per pound to producers, which is well above
the Fair Trade floor price. This minimum applies regardless of the market
price for green coffee in a given year, so producers are insulated from
price crashes. Aurora does not use long-term futures contracts; instead,
each relationship is renegotiated at the start of every harvest year based
on the previous year's cup quality and delivery reliability.

## Packaging and environmental impact

All Aurora bags use a compostable inner liner introduced in 2020, replacing
the previous foil-lined bags. The outer craft paper is sourced from
FSC-certified mills. Aurora's roastery runs partially on solar power
installed on the Central Eastside roastery's roof in 2022, covering roughly
a third of the facility's annual electricity use.

## Where Aurora is today

As of the most recent count, Aurora sources from twenty-six producer
partners across five countries and roasts every order to order rather than
holding pre-roasted inventory, which is why the 10:00 AM Pacific roasting
cutoff exists in the first place — same-day roasting only works because
Aurora doesn't roast ahead of demand. The company remains privately held and
has not raised outside venture funding; growth has been funded entirely
through revenue since the 2014 founding.
